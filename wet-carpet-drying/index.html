Wet Carpet Drying Help in Norwalk

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Norwalk extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
Our Process

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Swollen Subfloor

Particleboard and OSB under carpet swell and crumble when they stay wet. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Soaked Pad Underneath

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Mold on the Backing

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Water Quality Drops

Water that started clean does not stay that way. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.
