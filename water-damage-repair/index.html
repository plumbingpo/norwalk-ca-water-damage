Water Damage Repair, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage repair from start to finish:

1

Confirm It Is Dry

Final moisture readings are taken on the subfloor, framing and any drywall left in place. Until the readings are normal for every material, no reconstruction happens, and no moisture gets trapped.

2

Put the Scope in Writing

Each room is itemized in a document showing replacements, the materials chosen and how long we expect it to take. If a claim is open, we line the scope up with the adjuster.

3

Reconstruct and Install

Insulation goes back into open cavities, drywall is hung, taped and finished, and subfloor is repaired. After that we install trim, cabinetry and floors, ordering the work so completed surfaces are not damaged.

4

Final Finishes and Walkthrough

The last stage covers texture matching, primer, paint and putting hardware, doors and baseboards back. Together we then walk through each room, and every listed item is corrected before the job is closed.

Water Damage Repair Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • When kitchens, bathrooms or laundry rooms are rebuilt, choose backer board and moisture resistant drywall.
  • Fix the source first, and only then patch or paint a water stain.
  • Rebuilt bathroom walls last longer when the exhaust fan runs through and after each shower.
  • Curing time for paint, floor adhesive and caulk should pass before the room sees water or hard use.
  • Once a year, renew the caulk at sinks, showers and tubs to keep water from entering the wall.
  • Photograph damaged materials before they are removed in case your insurer asks later.

Signs You Need Water Damage Repair in Norwalk

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Hardwood boards have cupped, crowned or lifted and did not flatten after drying.
  • Flooring flexes more than it should in the spot that was flooded.
  • Cabinet bases or side panels are swollen, soft or peeling at the bottom.
  • You see brown stains, peeling or bubbles in paint along the path the water took.
  • Interior doors now stick or will not latch after swelling from moisture.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
  • During drying, sections of wallboard came out and the framing is still exposed.
