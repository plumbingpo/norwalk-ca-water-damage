Typical Water Damage Problems in San Diego

Most water damage calls we take in San Diego trace back to a short list of causes. If you can spot these early, you have a good chance of stopping the damage before it spreads.

Pipes: A pipe that freezes can split, and the flooding starts once the ice thaws and pressure returns. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: Washing machines, dishwashers and refrigerator ice makers all depend on small hoses that wear out. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. The crew from Norwalk Restoration can dry the attic and ceiling, but the roof itself needs a repair to stop the leak.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Carpet, wall studs and drywall wick moisture upward, which is why finished lower levels take the worst of it.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. Many mold jobs we handle in San Diego began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.

The First Hour After Water Damage in San Diego

People mean well in the first hour after a leak, and some of what they do makes things worse. These are the mistakes our crews see most often in San Diego, and what to do instead.

Never run an ordinary vacuum cleaner over a wet floor. You risk a shock and a dead vacuum, and a mop with old towels does the job safely. Leave ceiling fixtures off if the ceiling above is wet, and keep away from any part that droops. Do not lift tacked-down carpet yourself, since it can shrink or tear and may be saveable if it is handled properly. Avoid cranking up the heating, as warmth plus moisture encourages mold.

The helpful things are simple: close the valve, prop open cupboards and wardrobes, and stand wet cushions and textiles up to drain. Do take pictures before moving anything. Keep kids and animals out until it has been checked. Most of all, phone early, so a Norwalk Restoration crew is already heading for San Diego while you work through this list.
